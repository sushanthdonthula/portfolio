About the ISM Program

Frisco ISD’s Independent Study and Mentorship (ISM) program is a rigorous course for which high-achieving students apply, interview, and earn recommendations during their junior or senior year. Students focus their study on a self-selected career topic, conducting professional interviews and developing an extensive research portfolio.


Throughout the course, students refine time management, public speaking, research, and soft skills. During the spring semester, students collaborate with a professional mentor in their field of interest to gain real-world experience and build an advanced final product presented in May.

Assessment #1: Industry Trends & Future Outlook

AI Data Center Power: Grid Limits Reshape Energy in 2026

Investigated how global AI energy demands are driving a projected 26% increase in energy consumption. Evaluated hardware bottlenecks, heat dissipation constraints, and the industry shift from monolithic scaling to 2.5D/3D chiplet packaging architectures.


Assessment #2: Annotated Bibliography

  • GaN Power Devices for AI Data Centers: Evaluated Gallium Nitride (GaN) power converter architectures for thermal density and power efficiency.
  • Theory of Deep Learning: Studied mathematical frameworks for "learning mechanics" in neural networks across diverse architectures.
  • Lensless Imaging Systems: Analyzed mask-based computational encoders used to capture visual data without traditional physical lenses.
  • Monolayer WSe2 p-Type FETs: Examined strain engineering in 2D p-type transistors to boost hole mobility as a potential silicon replacement.
  • Thermal Latch in GaN Multichannel Devices: Reviewed sub-60-mV/dec switching behavior engineered through micro-fin thermal latching for radiofrequency applications.

Assessment #3: Advanced Transistor Fabrication (2D FETs)

Assessment #4: Numerical Homogenization & Thermal Modeling

[ View Kami Annotations ]
